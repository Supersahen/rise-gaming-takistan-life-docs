# Economy Overview

Takistan Life runs on interconnected economic systems.

Understanding how money is generated, lost, and redistributed will reduce early frustration and improve decision-making.

This guide explains how the systems function.

---

## 1. Types of Income

### A. Legal Income (Low Risk)

These systems generate steady money with limited player conflict:

- Mining and refining
- Legal crop harvesting
- Territory income (BLUFOR / OPFOR)
- ESU revives

Legal income is consistent and suitable for early progression.

---

### B. Illegal Income (High Risk)

These systems generate higher payouts but trigger faction response:

- Drug production and sale
- Meth production
- Robberies (bank, casino, gas station, farmers market, pharmacy)
- Kidnapping and ransom
- Territory disruption

Illegal income increases exposure to:

- Police patrols
- Military response
- Player retaliation

Higher reward increases operational risk.

---

### C. Faction-Level Income

These systems affect map control and faction stability:

- Territory captures
- Convoy operations
- Border enforcement
- Contract work (PMC)

Faction-level income impacts equipment access, vehicle usage, and map pressure.

---

## 2. How Money Is Lost

Money can be lost through:

- Carrying cash and dying.
- Bank robberies (if uninsured).
- Losing vehicles containing cargo.
- Failed high-risk operations.

Minimise exposure before engaging in risk.

---

## 3. Bank vs Cash

- Banked money is protected on death.
- Cash carried is lost on death.
- Bank robberies can affect stored balances.
- Bank insurance prevents robbery losses.
- Insurance can be carried or stored privately.

Deposit excess cash before high-risk activity.

---

## 4. Logistics and Scaling

Transport capacity directly affects profitability.

- Larger vehicles increase efficiency.
- Storage upgrades increase survivability.
- Escorts reduce loss risk.
- Planning extraction routes reduces failure rate.

Poor logistics is the most common cause of economic loss.

---

## 5. Territory Impact

Territory control affects:

- Government income generation
- Patrol mission locations
- Frequency of conflict

Territory captures are not only combat events — they are economic systems.

If territory changes hands, income distribution shifts.

---

## 6. Escalation Pattern

Most major server conflicts follow a predictable structure:

1. High-value illegal activity begins.
2. Government factions respond.
3. Territory becomes contested.
4. Convoys or retaliation events occur.
5. Economic balance shifts.

Understanding this pattern allows better timing of operations.

---

## 7. Choosing an Economic Path

### New Players
Start with legal income and upgrade transport before scaling.

### Organised Civilian Groups
Secure capital, then expand into structured illegal operations.

### Government Factions
Maintain territory control and patrol stability.

### Insurgents
Disrupt high-value transport and destabilise territory strategically.

### PMC
Take contracts aligned with active economic pressure points.

---

## 8. Risk Management Principles

Before engaging in high-risk operations:

- Deposit excess cash.
- Confirm insurance is active.
- Ensure backup is available.
- Plan entry and exit routes.
- Confirm vehicle capacity is appropriate.

Avoid operating alone in high-value scenarios.

---

## Summary

The economy is built on:

- Controlled risk
- Logistics
- Territory influence
- Inter-faction pressure

Players who understand these systems progress faster and lose less.
