# OPFOR (TLA – Southern Authority)

**Role:** Military control, territorial enforcement, and southern governance.

OPFOR controls the South and operates as a structured military authority. You enforce your territory, conduct military operations, and support the Commander’s strategic objectives.

OPFOR is not a criminal faction. You operate under authority.

---

## Core Responsibilities

- Maintain control of southern territory.
- Conduct patrol and defense operations.
- Process arrests through the southern prison system.
- Defend government convoys.
- Support war operations when declared.
- Maintain economic stability in the South.

Territory control is a major income source for OPFOR. Patrol and defense missions frequently align with capture zones.

---

## Economic Operations (Legal)

Mining is one of OPFOR’s primary money makers.

OPFOR has access to:

- Platinum
- Diamonds
- Oil
- Gold
- Other southern resources

One refinery is located in the South, allowing OPFOR to process materials without crossing into the North.

Members may purchase and use mining equipment to generate steady income.

Economic strength supports military strength.

---

## First Deployment Priorities

1. Equip standard combat gear.
2. Operate in squads whenever possible.
3. Focus on patrol, mining, and territory defense early.
4. Confirm current war status before moving north.

Coordination is expected.

---

## Jurisdiction & War

- OPFOR controls the South.
- During peacetime, non-OSF personnel may not operate in the North without proper authorisation from BLUFOR.
- Only OSF members may conduct border incursions without prior BLUFOR permission, provided roleplay justification exists.
- The Commander may declare war to enable cross-border operations.

Border rules are strict and enforced.

---

## Arrests & Enforcement

OPFOR may:

- Arrest and process prisoners in the southern prison.
- Issue taxation penalties within rule limits.

Enforcement actions (restraining, charging, processing) are performed through the player interaction system.

---

## Territory Operations

- Southern territories generate income.
- Reclaim insurgent-held zones to stabilise the South.
- Patrol and defense missions commonly overlap capture zones.
- Avoid entering hostile zones without support.

Territory control is strategic, not optional.

---

## Equipment & Assets

OPFOR has access to:

- Armoured transports and logistics vehicles
- Military helicopters
- Combat-grade weapons and equipment
- Drone systems

War-designated vehicles are restricted outside declared war conditions.

---

## Drones & Airspace

OPFOR drones may operate in northern territory.

Cross-border drone activity is considered hostile and may provoke retaliation.

Use aerial assets strategically.

---

## Government Convoys

OPFOR government convoys deliver faction supplies.

- Convoys attract hostile attention.
- Escorts are recommended.
- Interception risk increases during war.

Convoy defense supports both morale and economy.

---

## OSF (Special Forces)

OSF is a whitelisted specialist unit within OPFOR.

- Conducts high-risk and cross-border operations.
- Requires application and approval.
- Operates under mission authority.

OSF is structured special operations — not independent roaming.

---

## Bank & Insurance

- Banked money is safe on death.
- Cash carried is not.
- During bank robberies, balances may be affected.
- Insurance protects against robbery loss.

Financial discipline matters.

---

## Vehicle Discipline

- Store vehicles correctly.
- Use heavy transport for prisoner movement.
- Escort high-value cargo.
- Return war-designated vehicles when required.

Logistics failures weaken authority.

---

## Restrictions

- OPFOR cannot harvest, process, or sell drugs.
- OPFOR cannot join civilian gangs.
- Border and jurisdiction rules must be followed strictly.

---

## Professional Expectations

OPFOR works best when:

- Territory is consistently defended.
- Mining and economy remain strong.
- Operations are coordinated.
- War is declared strategically.

Operate as a military authority — not as an unstructured firefight.
