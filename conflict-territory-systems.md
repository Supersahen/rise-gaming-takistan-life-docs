# Conflict & Territory Systems

This guide explains how border control, war status, and territory capture interact.

Understanding these systems prevents most faction disputes.

---

# 1. Border & Jurisdiction

The map is divided into two primary jurisdictions:

- **North** – Controlled by BLUFOR
- **South** – Controlled by OPFOR

Each faction enforces law within its own territory.

---

## Peacetime Border Rules

During peacetime:

- BLUFOR enforces the North.
- OPFOR enforces the South.
- Non-OSF OPFOR members may not enter the North without authorisation from BLUFOR.
- BLUFOR may not conduct operations in the South without proper conditions.
- Insurgents may operate across the map but remain hostile to both government factions.

Border violations are treated as escalation events.

---

# 2. War & Martial Law

War changes border restrictions.

- Only the OPFOR Commander may declare war.
- When war is declared, cross-border operations become permitted.
- Certain vehicles and equipment become available during war.
- When war ends, restrictions immediately return.

War is structured and strategic — not spontaneous.

---

## Martial Law

Martial law may alter enforcement rules and vehicle restrictions.

Vehicle and equipment limitations must be respected when martial law is lifted.

---

# 3. Drone & Airspace Rules

Drone usage is jurisdiction-based:

- BLUFOR drones may only operate in northern airspace.
- OPFOR drones may operate in northern territory, but cross-border activity may be considered hostile.
- Airspace violations can escalate conflict quickly.

Drone deployment should be deliberate.

---

# 4. Territory Capture Mechanics

Territories generate faction income.

Territory control affects:

- Government income
- Patrol mission locations
- Conflict pressure across the map

Territory is not only a combat objective — it is an economic system.

---

## How Territory Changes

Territory may change through:

- Capture mechanics
- Sustained faction presence
- Defense or reclaim operations

When a faction loses territory, its income stability is reduced.

---

# 5. Patrol & Defense Missions

Patrol and defense missions often overlap with capture zones.

For BLUFOR and OPFOR:

- Patrol missions move officers across the map.
- Defense missions reinforce active territory.
- A patrol into hostile territory may become a reclaim operation.

Territory and patrol systems are interconnected.

---

# 6. Economic Impact of Territory

Territory control influences:

- Government income stability
- Frequency of conflict
- Convoy vulnerability
- Strategic positioning

Insurgent disruption reduces stability.

Government control increases predictability.

---

# 7. Escalation Flow

Most cross-faction conflict follows this pattern:

1. Border violation or illegal activity occurs.
2. Government faction responds.
3. Territory becomes contested.
4. Reinforcements escalate engagement.
5. Economic impact follows.

Understanding this cycle prevents unnecessary escalation.

---

## Final Principle

Before crossing borders or initiating large-scale operations:

- Confirm current war status.
- Confirm jurisdiction.
- Coordinate with faction leadership.
- Understand territory implications.

Most disputes come from misunderstanding border systems.
